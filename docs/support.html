Geda Clipboard

Support

The questions that come up most, and where to ask one that does not.

Getting help

Report a bug or ask a question on the public issue tracker: github.com/thienanblog/geda-clipboard/issues. Please say which version of macOS you are on and which version of the app — you will find it under Preferences › About.

I opened the app and nothing happened

That is expected. Geda has no window of its own and no Dock icon. Look for its icon in the menu bar, at the top right of the screen, and click it. Or press ⇧⌘V from anywhere.

If you use Bartender, Ice, or another menu bar manager, a newly installed icon is usually hidden by default. Unhide "Geda Clipboard" there and it will appear.

The shortcut does not open the popup

  • Another app may already own ⇧⌘V. Pick a different combination in Preferences › Shortcut: click the shortcut field, then press the keys you want.
  • Check the app is actually running — its icon should be in the menu bar.
  • Turn on Launch at login in Preferences so it is always there.

Choosing an entry copies it but does not paste it

In the Mac App Store version this is how it works. Pressing ⌘V on your behalf requires Accessibility permission, which App Store rules reserve for apps that assist people with disabilities, so that version leaves the feature out. Choosing an entry copies it and puts you back in the app you were working in, and you press ⌘V yourself.

In the version downloaded from this site or installed with Homebrew, the app can paste for you once you grant the permission:

  1. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security › Accessibility.
  2. Turn on Geda Clipboard.

Preferences tells you when that permission is missing and offers a button that takes you straight there.

I get no notifications when I copy

Open System Settings › Notifications › Geda Clipboard and allow them. Preferences › Notifications has a Send a test notification button to confirm they are getting through. You can also turn the copy and paste alerts off independently there.

Will it record my passwords?

Not from a password manager. Those mark what they put on the clipboard as confidential, and the app skips anything marked that way — this is on by default under Preferences › Privacy. You can also list any application by name and the app will never record a copy from it.

Nothing recorded ever leaves your Mac. See the privacy policy.

Where is my history kept, and how do I delete it?

In the app's own sandboxed folder on your Mac. Delete a single entry with ⌥⌫, delete unpinned history with Clear history, or set a smaller limit under Preferences › Clipboard. Pinned entries are kept by default; Preferences › Pinned can include them in history clearing and also lets you arrange their priority. Statistics can be reset separately under Preferences › Statistics. Removing the app deletes the lot.

The search field keeps focus while the popup is open, so two of these defer to it when there is text to act on: ⌥⌫ deletes the previous word of a search you have typed rather than an entry, and ⌘C copies a selection in the field. With the search empty they act on the list.

System requirements

macOS 13 Ventura or later, on Apple silicon or Intel.
